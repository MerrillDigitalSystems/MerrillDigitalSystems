How people find a plumber

Nobody Searches for "Plumbing Company"

They search for the thing that just went wrong in their house.

The searches that turn into work are specific and panicked: burst pipe, water heater replacement, drain cleaning near me, sewer line repair, emergency plumber. Each one carries a different level of urgency and a wildly different ticket size, and each one deserves its own page. A single "Services" page listing eleven bullet points competes for none of them properly.

Emergency intent behaves unlike almost any other kind of local search. There is no comparison shopping, no reading three About pages, no filling out a quote form and waiting. The first credible result gets the call, and "credible" is decided in about four seconds: does this load, is there a phone number I can tap, do they cover my city, does this look like a real company. Design polish is far down that list. Speed, clarity, and a visible service area are the whole game.

Non-emergency work is the opposite. A homeowner pricing a water heater replacement or a whole-house repipe will visit four sites, compare warranties, and look for financing before anyone picks up a phone. Those visitors need depth: what the job involves, what it typically costs, what happens if something fails later. One site has to serve both behaviors without watering either down. The Utah market

What Utah Does to Plumbing

Three conditions shape demand along the Wasatch Front, and each one is a service line worth its own page.

Hard water. Water along the Wasatch Front is notoriously hard, and it shows up in your call log every week — scale-clogged water heaters failing early, fixtures and valves seizing, tankless units needing descaling, and a steady appetite for whole-house softeners. Most Utah plumbing sites bury water treatment in a footer list. That is a mistake. Softener installation and water heater flushing get searched on their own, they are planned purchases rather than emergencies, and they deserve real pages.

Winter freezes. Demand does not arrive evenly. The first hard freeze of the season produces a spike of frozen and burst pipe calls, concentrated into a handful of brutal days. Hose bibs, unheated crawl spaces, garage walls, and vacant rentals all fail at once. A site that already ranks for "frozen pipe" and "burst pipe repair" before November is capturing that spike; a site that starts publishing in December is not. We build the seasonal pages ahead of the season and keep them live year-round so they have time to earn their position.

Older housing stock. Established neighborhoods in Salt Lake City and Ogden are full of homes old enough to still have galvanized supply lines and clay or cast-iron sewer laterals. That drives the highest-ticket work in residential plumbing: repipes, sewer line replacement, trenchless lining, and camera inspections. These are researched jobs with long consideration windows, and they are exactly where content depth, financing options, and warranty language earn their keep.

Emergency mechanics

What Actually Wins the Emergency Call

Six things decide it, and only one of them is visual.

Click-to-Call Above the Fold

A real tel: link in the header and again in the hero, sized for a thumb, not a mouse. No contact form as the primary action on an emergency page — nobody types their address while water is spreading.

Honest After-Hours Language

"24/7" that rings to voicemail at 1am burns the lead and earns the review. If a live human answers overnight, say so plainly. If it is a two-hour callback window, say that instead — specific and true beats vague and impressive.

Upfront Pricing Transparency

State the diagnostic or trip fee, whether it is waived when work is approved, and whether you quote flat-rate before starting. Homeowners who have been burned once look for this before anything else on the page.

Service Area Stated Plainly

Named cities, on the page, in text — Sandy, Draper, Murray, West Jordan, Layton, Provo. Not a decorative map image Google cannot read and a panicked homeowner cannot interpret at a glance.

Sub-Two-Second Load on LTE

Emergency traffic is mobile traffic, often on a weak signal in a basement. We build hand-coded, image-optimized pages with no page-builder bloat, because a spinning loader in that moment is a lost job.

A Short Path for Non-Urgent Jobs

Water heater quotes, softener installs, and repipe estimates get a four-field form and an optional booking link, so the planned work converts without competing with the emergency call to action.

Structure

How We'd Lay Out a Utah Plumbing Site

A representative structure, not a description of a past plumbing client — we haven't built for a plumber yet, and we won't pretend otherwise.

Service pages, one per job type. Emergency plumbing, water heater repair and installation, drain cleaning, sewer line repair and replacement, whole-house repipe, and water softener installation. Six pages, each written for a different searcher with a different problem and a different budget. Each one carries its own headline, its own FAQs, and its own Service schema, so Google has a distinct thing to match against a distinct query instead of one generic page it has to guess about.

City pages for the towns you actually drive to. Salt Lake City, Sandy, Draper, West Jordan, Lehi, Ogden — whichever ones are genuinely inside your radius. These only work when they say something real: response times from your shop, the housing stock in that area, the neighborhoods you run in most. Thin pages that swap one city name for another get ignored by Google and insult the reader.

Schema that describes a plumbing business. Plumber and LocalBusiness markup on the site level with your address, phone, hours, and service area. Service markup on each service page. FAQPage markup on the questions homeowners actually ask. AggregateRating only if you have genuine reviews behind it. Schema is not a ranking trick; it removes ambiguity about what you do and where.

Internal links that connect the two axes. Every service page links to the cities it covers and every city page links back to the services offered there. That is what turns a folder of pages into something a search engine can navigate. It is the same structure behind results we've seen elsewhere — a bakery client indexed and ranking within three weeks, a home design client hitting 96/100/100/99 on PageSpeed after leaving Wix. Fixed price, agreed in writing before anything is built.

Plumbing sites run $2,000–$8,000. Around $2,000–$3,500 buys a tight five-to-seven page site: home, core services, service area, about, contact, with click-to-call and local SEO wired in. $4,000–$8,000 covers the full structure above — six or more dedicated service pages, several city pages, booking integration, financing and membership sections, and the content depth that high-ticket sewer and repipe work needs.

The useful comparison isn't another agency's quote. It's your own average ticket.
