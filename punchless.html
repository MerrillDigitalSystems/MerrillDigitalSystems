Why we, of all people, built a SaaS

We build software you own outright. Punchless is the deliberate exception.

Almost everything Merrill Digital builds is custom and yours the day it ships — no per-seat fees, no vendor lock-in, no subscription required to keep using your own system. That's the whole point of custom software.

So why does a studio that's practically anti-SaaS sell one? Because timecards are a universal problem, not a business-specific one. Every field service company on earth needs the same thing here: an honest record of who was where, and for how long. Billing a thousand companies for the same custom build would be wasteful — so we productized it once, and priced it so a two-truck shop can afford it.

How it works

Timecards that write themselves

No app to remember to open, no punch to forget. The geofence does the work.

01
Set a geofence
Draw a boundary around each job site and your shop. Punchless watches for crew phones crossing it — no beacons, no hardware, no manual clock.
02
Crews just work
When a tech arrives, the clock starts. When they leave, it stops. A pause button protects privacy off the clock. Managers watch it happen live on one dashboard.
03
Review the drafts
At period's end, every timecard is already drafted from GPS-verified times. You review, adjust, and approve — an audit trail sits behind each entry.
04
Run payroll
Approved hours export straight to QuickBooks, ADP, Gusto, Paychex, or CSV. Payroll becomes a review step, not a data-entry afternoon.
