The real problem

Nobody Goes Looking for "Job Management Software"

They go looking for a way to stop losing jobs between the office and the field.

The pattern is almost always the same. Jobs get booked in one place — a phone call, a form, a text to the owner. They get scheduled somewhere else, usually a shared calendar or a whiteboard. Crews find out what they are doing through a group text. Job notes come back as photos in someone's camera roll. Then at the end of the month, somebody rebuilds the whole picture in a spreadsheet so it can be invoiced.

Every one of those handoffs is a place where a job goes missing, gets billed wrong, or gets done twice. And none of them show up as a line item. You feel it as "we're busy but the margins are weird," or as the two evenings a week the owner spends reconciling what actually happened.

Job management software fixes that by making the job itself the single record — created once, visible to everyone who needs it, updated by the person doing the work, and turned into an invoice without re-entry. The question is only whether you buy that off a shelf or have it built. Here's how to tell when you've outgrown spreadsheets.

What we build

What a Job Management System Actually Includes

V1 covers the workflow you run every day. Everything else waits until it earns its place.

Job Records That Hold Everything

Customer, address, scope, quoted price, assigned crew, status, photos, notes, and change orders — on one record, in your terminology, with your job stages rather than a vendor's.

Scheduling & Crew Assignment

Assign jobs to people and days, see who is overloaded before the week starts, and handle the reschedules that actually happen — weather, a callback, a customer who is not home.

Field Updates From a Phone

Crews open their day, mark jobs started and finished, capture photos, and leave notes without calling the office. Managers see it as it happens instead of on Monday.

Invoicing Without Re-Entry

A finished job becomes an invoice. Two-way QuickBooks Online or Xero sync means nobody retypes anything, and payment status flows back onto the job record.

Role-Based Access

Owners see margin. Office staff see scheduling and billing. Crews see today's work and nothing else. Each role gets the screen that helps them, not a stripped-down version of yours.

Reporting You'll Actually Open

Job profitability, crew throughput, quote-to-close rate, what is sitting unbilled. Built from the questions you already ask, rather than a dashboard of whatever was easy to chart.

Build vs buy

When Off-the-Shelf Is the Right Answer

Sometimes it is, and we will say so. Here is the honest split — and a fuller version in our ServiceTitan comparison.

Buy off the shelf if…

  • ✓Your workflow already looks like how the platform expects you to work
  • ✓You need something running next week, not next quarter
  • ✓Headcount is stable, so per-seat pricing is predictable
  • ✓Standard reporting answers the questions you actually ask
  • ✓You would rather rent the problem than own the asset

Build custom if…

  • ✓Your team maintains spreadsheets alongside the software you pay for
  • ✓The feature you need is always one tier up
  • ✓Pay rules, job stages, or billing logic don't fit the vendor's model
  • ✓Growing the crew makes the software bill grow with it
  • ✓You want the system to be an asset you own, not a receipt
