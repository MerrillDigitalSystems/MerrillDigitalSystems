The SLC market

Competing in Utah's Toughest Local Search Market

You can't out-spend the big players here. You can out-build them.

Search any service keyword with "Salt Lake City" attached and you'll see the problem: national franchises with dedicated marketing teams, directory giants like Yelp and Angi holding two or three page-one slots, and established local companies that have been accumulating reviews for fifteen years. Nobody cracks that field with a template site and a claimed Google listing. What still works is technical precision — pages that load faster, answer the searcher's exact question, and carry cleaner structured data than the incumbents bothered to implement.

Geography is the other lever. "Salt Lake City" is really a chain of distinct markets running down the valley — Sugar House, Millcreek, Murray, Midvale, Sandy, Draper — plus West Valley and Taylorsville on the other side of I-15. A remodeler based in Sugar House and a plumber running routes out of Sandy are competing for different searches, and Google treats them differently in the map pack. We build page structures that match where you actually take jobs along that corridor instead of pointing one generic homepage at the entire valley.

Salt Lake also has more web agencies than anywhere else in the state, and most of them are built for retainer clients: discovery phases, account managers, monthly minimums. If you're a service business that needs a site that ranks and converts — not a six-month brand engagement — that model works against you. Ours is the opposite: fixed scope, fixed price, built personally by the founder, and you own every line of code and every account when it ships.
